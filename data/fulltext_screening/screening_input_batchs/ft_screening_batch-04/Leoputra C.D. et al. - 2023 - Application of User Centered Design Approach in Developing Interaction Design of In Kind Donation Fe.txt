Application of User-Centered Design Approach in 
Developing Interaction Design of In-Kind Donation 

Feature on a Crowdfunding Platform  

Christopher Davin Leoputra1, Dicky Prima Satya2, Muhammad Romadhon Al-ghazali3 

Information System and Technology 
Bandung Institute of Technology (ITB) 

Bandung, Indonesia 

Abstract—The rapid advancement of technology has had a 
profound impact on human life, driving the need for faster and 
more effective social activities, including donation practices. 
One notable example of technological progress is the 
emergence of social crowdfunding platforms, which have 
revolutionized the way donations are made. However, the 
integration of in-kind donation features, specifically donating 
goods, remains relatively limited on crowdfunding platforms, 
as 
they 
predominantly 
focus 
on 
managing 
monetary 
contributions. Nonetheless, there are segments of the 
population who require the donation of goods due to 
difficulties in managing unused items and a preference for 
ensuring that donated items serve their intended purpose. This 
background necessitates the development of an interaction 
design 
for 
incorporating 
the 
donation 
of 
goods 
on 
crowdfunding platforms. The primary objective of this 
research is to explore and identify the challenges and design 
considerations related to digital donation features for goods. 
Employing a user-centered design approach, based on the ISO 
9241-210:2019 method, the study follows a four-stage 
development 
process 
that 
prioritizes 
user 
needs 
and 
preferences. The culmination of this research effort is a high-
fidelity interactive prototype that achieves usability goals 
related to effectiveness and utility, as well as user experience 
goals associated with helpfulness and enjoyment. The 
evaluation of the prototype's effectiveness and user experience 
employs usability testing methods, including interviews, Single 
Ease Question (SEQ) surveys, and the System Usability Scale 
(SUS). The results of the second iteration of testing indicate 
that the constructed interaction design successfully addresses 
the predefined usability and user experience goals. Moreover, 
this phase of testing generates valuable insights that can inform 
future design recommendations. 

Keywords— in-kind donation, crowdfunding platform, 
interaction design, user-centered design, usability test.

II. LITERATURE REVIEW 

A. Interaction Design  
Interaction design is a multifaceted process encompassing 
the creation of interactive products aimed at facilitating 
human communication and interaction in daily life [3]. It can 
be perceived as an artistic endeavor that fosters seamless 
interaction between individuals through a diverse range of 
products and services (Saffer, 2010). At its core, interaction 
design integrates design principles that emphasize the 
effective presentation of information within systems, 
enabling users to comprehend complex information 
effortlessly [4], while ensuring that the interaction between 
users and products remains comprehensible without undue 
simplification [5]. 

From a scholarly standpoint, interaction design constitutes an 
integral component of the interdisciplinary field of user 
experience design, which underpins numerous scientific 
disciplines and primarily focuses on the analysis and design 
of computer-based systems [6]. Each discipline within the 
realm of interaction design is intricately interconnected with 
other interdisciplinary domains, fostering a comprehensive 
understanding of the intricate relationships between humans, 
technology, and design principles. 

The field of interaction design encompasses five dimensions 
that are crucial for understanding and modeling the 
involvement of interaction design in a product or system. 
Initially, the concept of these interaction design dimensions 
was introduced by Gillian Crampton Smith as four 
dimensions of interaction design language [7], which were 
later expanded with the addition of a fifth dimension by 
Kevin Silver from IDEXX Laboratories. These dimensions 
consists of words, visual representations, physical object or 
space, time, and behaviour provide a framework for 
analyzing and conceptualizing the various aspects of 
interaction design, enabling designers to create more holistic 
and effective interactive experiences. 

In addition to those five dimensions, there are several 
international laws that serve as guidelines and play a role in 

achieving the goals of interaction design in creating positive 
interactions. Some laws explain the principles of interaction 
design including Hick’s law, Fitt’s law, and Tesler’s law [8]. 

B. Nielsen’s Usability Principles 
Usability principles are a set of principles used to evaluate 
and enhance the usability of a system or product, as proposed 
by Jakob Nielsen and Rolf Molich (Nielsen, 1990). These 
principles aim to assist in the development, particularly in 
the context of interaction design. By applying these 
principles, the resulting products are expected to be more 
accessible, usable, and intuitive.  

Nielsen’s usability principles include Visibility of System 
Status, Match Between System and the Real World, User 
Control and Freedom, Consistency and Standards, Error 
Prevention, Recognition Rather than Recall, Flexibility and 
Efficiency of Use, Aesthetic and Minimalist Design, Help 
Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors, Help 
and Documentation. 

These principles serve as guidelines for designers to create 
user-friendly and effective interactive systems, ultimately 
improving the overall user experience. 

C. User Experience & Usability Goals 
According to ISO 9241-210, user experience (UX) is the 
perception and response of users resulting from the use of a 
product, system, or service. UX encompasses all aspects of 
end-users' interaction with a company, its services, and its 
products (Nielsen, 2017). 

One cannot directly design user experience but can design a 
product that can generate a user experience. User experience 
goals represent a spectrum of emotions and experiences 
perceived by users based on specific aspects [3]. These 
aspects can be categorized into desired aspects and undesired 
aspects, each influencing the overall user experience.

In general, usability is an attribute of quality used to evaluate 
the ease of interaction. According to ISO 9241-11, usability 

refers to the extent to which a product can be used by

Usability goals are related to the usefulness and ease of use 
of a product or system. In determining usability goals, there 
are six established standards: Easy to learn, Safe to use, 
Effective to use, Efficient to use, Good utility, and 
Memorable [10].

Octopus is a circular economy platform that enables

Indonesia introduces innovative approaches and modern 

technologies to revolutionize waste management, building 
upon traditional practices of waste collection, sorting, and 
recycling.

DonateStuff.com is an online platform based in the United

4. Share at Door Step 

SADS India (Share at Door Step) is an India-based online 
donation platform dedicated to assisting individuals in need 
within the country. It serves as a platform for receiving 
donations of various items, including clothing, food, 
medicine, books, and other usable goods, which are sent

III. METHODOLOGY 

Various methodologies commonly employed in interaction 
design include user-centered design, activity-centered design, 
genius design, and design sprint. The utilization of the user-
centered design method in this research is intrinsically linked 
to the inherent characteristics of user-centered design itself, 
which entails a collection of processes that are focused on 
placing users at the core of design and product development, 
with the aim of generating products that are in line with the 
needs and expectations of end-users (Abras, Maloney-
Krichmar, Preece, 2004). Human-centered design is a 
philosophy and a set of procedures that commence with a 
deep understanding of individuals as users and their needs, 
which must be addressed by design [12] .Norman perceives 
it as synonymous with user-centered design and asserts that 
the creation of exceptional, successful, and customer-
preferred products is highly feasible through the adoption of 
this approach. 

According to Jennifer Peerce, Hellen Sharp, and Yvonne 
Rogers in their book titled "Interaction Design: Beyond 
Human-Computer 
Interaction," 
user-centered 
design 
encompasses four flows and activities. In this study, the user-
centered design method employed adhered to the standards 
set by the International Standardization Organization (ISO), 

specifically ISO 9241-210:2019 on human-centered design 
for interactive systems. Similar to the stages defined by 
Peerce, ISO 9241-210:2019 delineates the user-centered 
design process into four phases, commencing with the 
planning of the design process with a human/user focus. 

 

Fig. 1. User-centered design ISO 9241-210:2019 

A. Context of Use 
During this stage, the focus is on comprehending the user 
characteristics and the extent of system utilization, which 
involves the identification of users, their system objectives, 
their behavioral patterns while interacting with the system, 
and the delineation of the system's usage boundaries. 

B. Specify Requirements 
During this phase, research is conducted to further refine the 
context of system usage that has been defined in the previous 
stage. This research involves surveying users to validate the 
identified issues (user validation). This stage focuses on 
formulating the requirements and system needs that must be 
fulfilled in relation to the context of system usage, as well as 
determining the expectations to be achieved in terms of UX 
and usability goals.. 

C. Design Solution 
During this phase, an iterative process is employed to 
develop the interaction design prototype, beginning with the 
construction 
of 
low-fidelity 
designs 
derived 
from 
rudimentary 
concepts, 
and 
advancing 
towards 
a 
comprehensive design that effectively illustrates the system's 
functionality, satisfying all user-defined specifications and 
requirements. 

D. Evaluate Against Requirements 
During this stage, the design solution undergoes testing by 
engaging actual users with the aim of ensuring the attainment 
of predetermined UX and usability goals. The evaluation is 
conducted not only to assess the functional aspects of the 
system but also to gather user input regarding the fulfillment 
of requirements in the proposed design solution. The testing 
outcomes and user input are subsequently analyzed and 
utilized as the foundation for enhancing the design solution, 
ultimately yielding a comprehensive design solution that 
satisfies all user needs.

D. Second Iteration 
The second testing phase was conducted with twenty 
participants who had previously participated in the first 
iteration of testing. This was done to provide a clearer picture 
of the improvements or progress made in the tested design 
solution and to re-evaluate the identified issues from the first 
iteration. Additionally, testing the updated version with 
participants from the first round ensured that the 
implemented improvements had indeed enhanced the 
usability and user experience of using the in-kind donation 
feature on the crowdfunding platform.

in the second iteration of testing was 6.80, showing an 
improvement of 0.41 points compared to the first iteration. 
The scores in the second iteration were also above average, 
and improvements were observed across all tasks. The 
highest score was achieved in Task 5 with a perfect score of 
7, while the lowest score was in Task 7 with a score of 6.63.

V. CONCLUSION 

According to the extensive research process and iterative 
design approach conducted in this study, titled "Development 
of Interaction Design for In-Kind Donation Feature on 
Crowdfunding Platform 
Using User-Centered Design 
Approach," it is concluded that this study successfully used a 
user-centered design approach to create a mobile web 
interaction design for in-kind donations on a crowdfunding 
platform. It involved extensive research, including user 
research with 108 participants and iterative design 
refinements with 20 users. The integration of the in-kind 
donation feature was justified for its potential to boost user 
acquisition and expand donation options. The evaluation 
confirmed that the design met usability goals and garnered 
high user satisfaction, emphasizing the importance of user 
involvement in designing user-friendly interactive systems 
on crowdfunding platforms.
